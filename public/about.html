About

A small desk, run on purpose

videoslocal.store is a transfer desk for video you have the right to keep. It is one person, a static page and a very good command-line tool.

What it is

The desk takes a link, resolves it with yt-dlp, shows you the spec sheet — source, title, duration, available formats — and then saves the file with ffmpeg doing the muxing. Resolution, format and container are your choice, not an algorithm's.

Nothing is uploaded. There is no account, no queue in the cloud, no copy of your file on a server somewhere. The engine runs where the files land, and the download folder is cleared on a timer.

Why it exists

Because the alternatives are unpleasant in three predictable ways. Public download sites are ad walls that proxy your traffic through someone else's infrastructure. Browser extensions ask for permission to read every page you visit. Screen recording is a lossy compromise that produces a worse file than the one you started with.

A local desk is the boring answer: one process, one folder, one log line per transfer. It is also the only version of this tool that does not need to know who you are.

What it will not do

It will not bypass DRM, guess credentials, or fetch something the platform is contractually required to gate. Subscription services encrypt their streams and licence the keys to approved players; this desk declines those sources rather than pretending otherwise. It will not batch-scrape a catalog, either — the rate limits are real, and a pattern that looks like abuse gets the desk throttled.

How it is funded

Advertising, served by Google, on the pages where it does not interrupt the work. No accounts to sell, no analytics profile to compile, no data broker in the chain. If advertising is turned off in your browser or declined in the consent panel, the desk works exactly the same.

Editorial standard

The guides are written to be checkable: a rule as written, a command you can run, an error you can reproduce. We avoid confident legal claims, name the jurisdiction when it matters, and mark opinion as opinion. Where we get something wrong, we correct the page and note the correction rather than editing it away.

Who runs it

An independent developer. No company behind the curtain, no parent platform, no affiliate network steering the recommendations. Correspondence goes to a human who reads it.
